What are these dashboards showing?

These dashboards turn large public datasets into charts you can explore. You can use the buttons above to switch between the two topics.

🇵🇭 Immigrant statistics

Each entry represents a registered Filipino emigrant, along with the year and place connected to that entry. The charts show changes over time and which regions, provinces, or municipalities have the most records.

⚡ Fuel-use analysis

These charts show how the Philippines generated electricity from different fuel sources. GWh is a unit for measuring electricity, and renewable share is the percentage produced by sources such as hydro, geothermal, solar, wind, and biomass.

📊 1. The Global Peak of 2006–2007

The dataset shows a distinct peak in emigration around 2006 (82,967 emigrants) and 2007 (80,599 emigrants). This trend is highly correlated with the global economic boom preceding the 2008 global financial crisis. High labor demand and established immigration visas in destination countries (primarily the United States, Canada, and Australia) combined with domestic pushes in the Philippines to facilitate legal emigration pathways for families and professionals. Following the financial crisis, immigration regulations tightened, leading to a noticeable drop in subsequent years.

🌎 2. Centralization and Geographic Concentration

There is a massive geographic imbalance in where registered emigrants originate. The National Capital Region (NCR) alone accounts for 687,518 emigrants (approx. 31.5% of the national total). When combined with the adjacent regions of Central Luzon and CALABARZON, the three regions account for nearly 64% of all emigrants. This centralization is largely driven by urbanization, density of visa centers/embassies, international transport access, and higher concentrations of recruitment and administrative resources compared to outer rural regions (like MIMAROPA, ARMM, and Caraga).

🦠 3. The 2020 COVID-19 Trough

The sharpest single-year decline occurred in 2020, where emigration plummeted to 15,703 (a 75.9% decline from 2019's 65,164). This is the lowest emigration count in the entire 32-year history recorded in this dataset and is directly caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, which resulted in travel bans, flights cancellations, border locks, and administrative shutdowns of processing entities globally.
